Abstract

This report documents the development of indicium-adapters-transcriptomics, a domain adapter for transcriptomics-claim enrichment in the AgenticScienceBuilder (ASB) knowledge-graph pipeline. The adapter implements two siblings, BulkTranscriptomicsAdapter and SingleCellTranscriptomicsAdapter, both conformant with the indicium core DomainAdapter protocol. Across versions 0.4.0 to 0.11.0 the adapter grew from a scaffolding stage to an end-to-end pipeline that ingests repository metadata (GEO SOFT, ENA XML, BioStudies JSON, ISA-Tab), enriches free-text claims with CURIEs drawn from a 19-source concept index (~447 verified entries spanning EFO, OBI, NCIT, STATO, GO, CL, UBERON, ChEBI, HP, MP, DOID, PR, OBA, ENVO, MS, UO, MOD, FBbt, WBbt, ZFA, EMAPA, XPO and bio.tools), and resolves gene symbols across six model organisms from a bundled 254,922-row index built from HGNC, MGI, FlyBase, WormBase, ZFIN and the Nematostella vectensis G2V2 cross-reference. Symbol-level collisions across species are disambiguated by an optional species claim hint and a casing heuristic (UPPER for human, Title-case for mouse, the xxx-N pattern for C. elegans). A study-versus-feature qualifier-aware resolver chain prevents the well-known STAR aligner / steroidogenic acute regulatory protein gene collision. End-to-end validation on a 16-paper hand-curated corpus (45 claims) yields 98% enrichment with 18 distinct ontologies firing and full SHACL conformance.

1. Introduction

1.1 Context

Knowledge-graph (KG) construction from biomedical literature increasingly relies on large language models (LLMs) for fact extraction. The AgenticScienceBuilder (ASB) framework, developed in our laboratory, evaluates such pipelines on a corpus of biomedical and transcriptomics studies. ASB ingests claims, that is triples of the form (subject, qualifier, object), validates them against domain-specific SHACL shapes, and stores them as nanopublications in a triplestore.

For the system to interoperate with downstream tools, free-text mentions in claims must be lifted to canonical CURIEs (Compact URIs) drawn from community-curated ontologies and registries. The indicium core provides the DomainAdapter protocol that domain-specific resolvers must implement; indicium-adapters-transcriptomics is the implementation for transcriptomics.

1.2 Goals

The adapter is designed to fulfil three roles, derived from the four-quadrant filter that governs scope decisions in the project:

  1. Constrain the LLM extraction stage, by exposing a closed set of domain qualifiers (differentially_expressed_in, marker_of_cell_type, sequenced_on, and so on) and an extraction-context prompt.
  2. Resolve free-text identifiers to canonical CURIEs (gene symbols to HGNC, accessions to GEO, instrument names to EFO, cell types to CL, and so on).
  3. Validate claim outputs against SHACL shapes, in particular the CURIE-format NodeShape shared across siblings.

The fourth quadrant, behaviours an LLM could perform as well freehand, is explicitly out of scope.

1.3 Sibling context

The package is one of three sibling adapters in the indicium ecosystem: indicium-adapters-transcriptomics (this work), indicium-adapters-metabolomics, and indicium-adapters-epigenomics. The three share the DomainAdapter contract and partition the qualifier vocabulary so that compose_adapters produces a clean union without shadowing.

2. Architecture

2.1 Two siblings, one composed surface

The package exports two adapter classes registered under the indicium.adapters entry-point group:

  • BulkTranscriptomicsAdapter handles bulk RNA-seq, microarray and time-series expression claims plus study-level assay metadata. Its 13 qualifiers span feature-level claims and study-level qualifiers such as sequenced_on, aligned_with, quantified_with.
  • SingleCellTranscriptomicsAdapter handles cell-resolution claims (cell-type markers, cluster enrichment, spatial localisation). Its 5 qualifiers are disjoint from those of the bulk adapter, an invariant enforced by tests/test_composition.py.

Both adapters share the TRANSCRIPTOMICS_PREFIXES CURIE-prefix map, the GENE_INDEX, ANATOMY_INDEX and CONCEPT_INDEX singletons, the resolver helpers, and the SHACL shapes graph.

2.2 Resolver dispatch

Both adapters use a uniform dispatcher pattern (introduced in v0.4.0). Each resolver accepts (name: str, species: str | None = None) and returns (curie, xrefs) | None. The enrich_claim method walks resolvers in a per-qualifier priority order:

2.3 Priority orders

Qualifier classPriority chainRationale
Default (feature claims)accession > gene > conceptGene mentions dominate feature claims; accessions are unambiguous and resolve first.
Study-level (sequenced_on, aligned_with, quantified_with, etc.)concept > accession > geneObject slot is typically a tool / platform / database. Resolves the STAR collision in favour of the aligner.

2.4 Species-aware lookup (gene resolver)

The gene resolver disambiguates symbol-level collisions (MAPK1 for human vs Mapk1 for mouse, both lower-cased to mapk1) using three rules in cascade:

  1. Explicit hint. If the claim carries a species field, the resolver filters candidates to that species first.
  2. Casing heuristic. UPPER-case symbols default to human, Title-case to mouse, the xxx-N pattern (three lowercase letters, hyphen, alphanumeric tail) to C. elegans. Lower-case is intentionally ambiguous and falls through to the load-order rule, preserving v0.1.x case-insensitive forgiveness for human-symbol typos.
  3. Cross-listed fallback. A Nematostella row carrying a human HGNC ortholog also serves human-species lookups. Without this rule, a casing-heuristic vote for "human" on MMP9 would miss because the only matching row is tagged nematostella.

3.3 Anatomy index

A 4-entry hand-curated UBERON anatomy index, built by data/build_anatomy_index.py with strict OLS4 label-match verification (no hyponym promotion). Coverage focuses on the Cheung 2024 Tomo-seq context: mesentery, pharynx, body wall, organism subdivision. Cnidarian-specific concepts (physa, mesoglea, gastrodermis, oral / aboral pole, body column, polyp body form) are deliberately absent because UBERON has no entry for them; they are listed in TRIAGED_OUT_KNOWN_CNIDARIAN for transparency.

4. Resolution methods

4.1 OLS4 harvester cascade

Concept indexes are populated by a per-axis cascade of three match modes implemented in data/build_corpus_concept_candidates.py:

  1. Exact. Label or synonym equals the corpus term verbatim (case-insensitive). Default for axes where false positives would be harmful (software tools, statistical tests).
  2. Prefix. The corpus term is the whole-word prefix of the OLS4 label. Lets descriptive suffixes through, so Wilcoxon resolves to Wilcoxon Signed Rank Test, Visium to Visium Spatial Gene Expression.
  3. Substring. The corpus term appears as a whole-word substring of the label. Used for instrument families whose canonical EFO label prepends a vendor name (NovaSeq → Illumina NovaSeq 6000).

The cascade is tried depth-first across the axis's preferred ontology list: a strict match in any preferred ontology wins over a relaxed match in the first ontology. A label-first refinement (v0.5.0) ensures that when OLS4's relevance ranking puts a synonym-match doc ahead of a label-match doc (the macrophage → plasmatocyte case), the canonical label-match still wins.

4.2 bio.tools harvester

Bioinformatics software is not consistently indexed in OBO-Foundry ontologies. The bio.tools registry fills that gap. data/build_biotools_index.py tries direct-ID lookup first (lowercased, spaces to hyphens), then a search fallback with exact name match. A normalised name match catches stylistic variants such as Bowtie2 ↔ Bowtie 2.

4.3 Metadata parsers

The parsers/ subpackage provides four functions sharing a uniform input → list[dict] contract. Each emits study-level claims directly consumable by enrich_claim:

  • parse_geo_soft(text). GEO SOFT format. Tokenises ^TYPE = id records and !field = value lines, walks ^SERIES and ^SAMPLE records, emits consolidates_dataset per sample id, deduplicated sequenced_on per instrument model, and uses_database PubMed:{id} when the PubMed reference is set.
  • parse_ena_xml(xml_text). ENA / SRA XML. Walks EXPERIMENT elements, links to STUDY_REF, reads the vendor-named child of PLATFORM for INSTRUMENT_MODEL.
  • parse_biostudies_json(text_or_obj). BioStudies JSON. Flattens the section.attributes tree into a name to value dict, emits study-level claims under the document accno.
  • parse_isa_tab(directory). ISA-Tab investigation, study, assay bundle. Reads i_*.txt for Study Identifier and Study PubMed ID, walks s_*.txt and a_*.txt for organism and instrument-model columns.

Each parser detects a recognised organism name in the relevant field and propagates a species hint on every emitted claim, so the gene resolver picks the correct organism downstream without depending on symbol casing.

7. Limitations

7.1 Cross-organism anatomy collisions

Some anatomical terms appear in multiple species-specific anatomies (for example, somite in both EMAPA and ZFA). The loader's priority ladder loads EMAPA before ZFA, so a bare somite claim resolves to the mouse entry. A qualifier-aware species priority for anatomy, analogous to what is already done for gene resolution, would address this. It is deferred to a future release as the workaround (pass species hint) suffices in practice.

7.2 Ontologies with poor OLS4 fit

Four ontologies from the v0.8.0 audit short-list were deferred at v0.9.0 due to label-availability issues, each documented in data/build_extra_ontology_indexes.py:

  • EC. The OLS4 ec ontology re-exports GO molecular-function terms without human-readable labels. Cascade label-matching cannot catch common enzyme class names. Future versions can wire ExPASy enzyme.dat directly.
  • RXNO. Dominated by CHEBI and BFO imports without named reaction labels.
  • HCAO. Re-publishes CARO, CL, CLO and GO terms. Redundant with the existing CL index.
  • ZP. Labels follow a peculiar <entity> abnormal, abnormal pattern that does not match the natural-language abnormal <entity> form of harvest candidates. A pattern-aware harvester is needed.

7.3 Protein Ontology generic-candidate quirks

The PR harvest at v0.8.0 contains valid CURIEs that are not always the most canonical: transcription factor resolves to a specific Sp-family transcription factor, actin to actin (yeast). The label-first cascade refinement could be extended to prefer the shortest label among label-matches; this is queued for a future PR-targeted refinement.

7.4 Instrument full-name extensions

The corpus index has bare instrument-series entries (NovaSeq, HiSeq, NextSeq) that match substring. Full product names with model numbers (Illumina HiSeq 2500, Illumina NextSeq 500) do not exact-match. Either the corpus index can grow with more variants, or upstream LLM extractors can normalise to bare series names.

7.5 Single bio.tools miss

scVI is not registered in bio.tools under that name, leading to consistent unresolved claims in scRNA-seq papers that use the tool. Adding a direct-ID attempt for the canonical scvi-tools identifier is a one-line fix queued for the next bio.tools harvest refresh.

8. Future work

Three threads remain open beyond the v0.11.0 baseline:

  1. Real-world benchmark on the ASB pipeline. The 16-paper hand-curated corpus exercises the adapter's resolution paths end to end, but the next milestone is integration with the ASB LLM extraction stage in the laboratory's downstream infrastructure. The coverage matrix from v0.11.0 provides the baseline against which extraction-stage misses can be diagnosed.
  2. Niche ontology additions. The remaining 21 candidates from the v0.8.0 OLS4 audit (BTO, OGSF, CMPO, MAXO, OMIT, NBO, MEDDRA and others) can be added incrementally as benchmark output surfaces missing terms. The infrastructure is in place; the cost per addition is approximately one hour.
  3. SHACL pipeline shapes. The current shapes graph validates CURIE format only. Class-level shapes for SO, UBERON, CL, EFO would add semantic validation. Such shapes typically live in the consuming graph store rather than in the adapter itself, but the contract for what the adapter emits could be made stricter.
